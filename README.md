# CS 250 - Software Development Lifecycle

## Course Portfolio Reflection

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250 at Southern New Hampshire University. Throughout this course, I gained a better understanding of Agile development, Scrum practices, teamwork, and the importance of meeting user needs.

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I believe understanding user needs is one of the most important parts of software development. Before creating a program, developers should understand what users expect and how the software will help them. User stories make this process easier by breaking down requirements into smaller, manageable tasks. During our SNHU Travel project, I learned how user stories helped the team understand expectations and prioritize features. I also learned that communicating with the Product Owner and gathering feedback throughout development can prevent misunderstandings and help create a better experience for users.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing programs, I prefer an organized approach that allows room for adjustments and improvements. Throughout this course, I learned that Agile development helps teams identify problems early instead of waiting until the end of a project. In our slideshow project, we encountered issues with images and pages not displaying correctly, which showed me the importance of testing and communication. In my future development work, I would like to incorporate sprint planning, daily stand-up meetings, regular testing, and retrospectives. I believe these practices improve teamwork, efficiency, and software quality.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, being a good team member means communicating effectively, being dependable, and supporting others throughout the development process. Software development requires collaboration between developers, testers, Scrum Masters, and Product Owners. During this course, I learned how important it is to share progress updates, ask questions, and address problems before they become bigger issues. I believe being open to feedback, respecting different perspectives, and helping teammates creates a stronger and more productive development team. As I continue pursuing a career in software engineering, I want to bring these qualities into every team I work with.
